# SEO audit: Drew’s Pet Care

Audited November 9, 2026. Production: <https://drewspets.com>.
Priority: pet-sitting and dog-walking inquiries in **Fox River Grove and Cary**.

## Executive summary

**The live site has sound crawlability and on-page foundations. No critical technical indexing blocker was found in the tested URLs.** All 29 sitemap pages return HTTP 200, have unique titles and descriptions, use self-canonicals, and allow indexing. This establishes technical eligibility, not actual Google indexation or rankings.

The most useful next work is to establish search and inquiry performance, tighten the main service snippets, and make service choices more useful to local owners. Local reputation work may have substantial value, but Business Profile completeness and review performance were not accessible and are not reported as defects.

Top priorities:

1. Verify actual Google indexing and query performance for the two core towns.
2. Establish organic landing-page-to-inquiry measurement; Vercel Analytics is enabled in the deployed bundle, while GA4 initialization is absent.
3. Shorten the core service descriptions and replace literal “near me” phrasing with clear service-and-town wording.
4. Distinguish house sitting from overnight dog sitting and answer practical service questions with confirmed operating facts.
5. Check the existing Google Business Profile and build genuine customer proof and local mentions.

Quick wins are metadata edits and clearer service labels. There is no evidence here supporting a site rebuild, additional town pages, or a mass publishing campaign.

## Scope and evidence

- Reviewed repository routes, SEO helpers, content, analytics implementation, blog publishing workflow, and existing organic-discovery documentation. No product-marketing context file was found in the repository.
- Requested every URL in the live sitemap: 29 pages, including eight service pages, ten town pages, a pricing guide, and one blog post plus its index.
- Checked `/apply`, `/refer`, four deliberately missing URLs, RSS, the Open Graph image response, and `/book?service=dog-walking`.
- Checked HTTP and HTTPS on apex and www, on both the homepage and the booking query URL.
- Parsed response HTML for metadata, headings, internal links, image attributes, and JSON-LD. Inspected the deployed analytics JavaScript to distinguish client-loaded analytics from an absent HTML script tag.
- Ran public discovery searches for `site:drewspets.com`, the quoted domain, and the business name with Fox River Grove. These did not surface the domain in the tool’s returned results. This is a visibility observation, not proof that Google has indexed zero pages. Google explicitly documents that [site searches are not exhaustive](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-operators/all-search-site).
- The connected OpenSEO account lists three projects, none for this domain. No Search Console, GA4, Vercel Analytics dashboard, Business Profile performance, or backlink dataset was available for this site through the checked connection.

The accompanying [page inventory](seo-audit-2026-11-09-pages.csv) records live status, titles, descriptions, canonicals, headings, and JSON-LD types for each sitemap URL.

## Technical SEO findings

### T1. Actual indexing and search performance need verification

**Impact:** High if priority pages are excluded. **Priority:** P1 verification. **Status:** Unknown, not a confirmed indexing failure.

**Evidence:** The 29 pages are technically indexable, but authenticated index coverage and Google-selected canonicals were unavailable. Public search did not establish visibility. Verification tags alone would not settle ownership because a property can use DNS verification.

**Fix:** In the existing Search Console property, inspect `/`, `/locations/fox-river-grove-il`, `/locations/cary-il`, `/services/dog-walking`, `/services/overnight-dog-sitting`, `/services/cat-sitting`, and `/guides/pet-sitting-rates`. Record indexing status, last crawl, Google-selected canonical, and exclusions. Confirm the sitemap is submitted and processed. Resolve any actual exclusion before expanding content. Export the last 28 days of queries and landing pages with a previous-period comparison; prioritize queries already near page one once data supports that choice.

### T2. Organic inquiry measurement is incomplete or unverified

**Impact:** High for prioritization and attribution; no direct ranking claim. **Priority:** P1 measurement.

**Evidence:** [AnalyticsProvider](../components/site/analytics.tsx) conditionally enables Vercel Analytics and GA4. The live chunk `2kh6frv7pcvrc.js` mounts Vercel Analytics, while the GA script branch is compiled to an empty string. Booking code calls `booking_request_submitted` after a successful response, but this audit did not submit a real inquiry or verify event receipt. GA4 absence does not imply zero measurement: Vercel reporting may already contain useful data.

**Fix:** Review Vercel traffic and custom-event reporting first. If GA4 is the chosen attribution tool, configure the real `NEXT_PUBLIC_GA_ID`, redeploy, verify page views and successful inquiry events, and mark `booking_request_submitted` as a key event. Follow the existing [measurement instructions](organic-discovery.md) to avoid duplicate history-based page views. Review organic landing pages and manually reconcile successful inquiries with actual clients. Do not send customer names, email addresses, phone numbers, or pet-care notes to analytics.

### T3. Performance and mobile experience remain unmeasured

**Impact:** Unknown. **Priority:** P2 verification, with remediation based on results.

**Evidence:** PageSpeed Insights API returned HTTP 429 `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`; no Lighthouse or CrUX metrics were obtained. Source and response HTML show responsive viewport metadata, responsive Next image URLs, image dimensions or reserved fill containers, lazy loading on lower images, and a preloaded hero. Those are useful implementation choices, not proof of passing Core Web Vitals.

**Fix:** Run mobile PageSpeed Insights for the homepage, dog-walking page, and Cary page, and review Search Console’s field data when available. Check actual LCP, INP, CLS, image requests, mobile overflow, navigation, and form usability. Investigate the measured bottleneck before changing fonts, images, or animation. No mobile layout or browser interaction pass is claimed by this audit.

### Technical checks that passed

- `/robots.txt` allows public pages and blocks `/api/`; it references the correct apex sitemap.
- All 29 sitemap URLs return 200 with self-canonicals and no page-level noindex. Their titles and descriptions are unique.
- Every sitemap page has exactly one H1 in the returned HTML. Every sitemap page has at least one incoming internal link; all discovered same-origin page destinations resolve within the tested set.
- `/apply` and `/refer` are intentionally noindex and excluded from the sitemap.
- Unknown root, service, town, and blog paths return real 404 responses. Although layout metadata is also present, a noindex directive is included; this is not a soft-404 finding.
- HTTP redirects to HTTPS; www redirects to the apex. Booking paths and query strings survive. HTTP www takes two permanent redirects, a minor optimization opportunity rather than an indexing blocker.
- HTTPS responses include HSTS. No mixed-content URLs were identified in the inspected page references.
- All inspected image elements have alt attributes. Decorative brand portraits correctly use empty alt text. Responsive image URLs are present; actual transfer sizes and browser-selected widths were not measured.
- Business/person/site, service, town WebPage, breadcrumb, FAQ, Article, and BlogPosting JSON-LD are present where applicable and parse as JSON. This is positive source/response evidence, not a rendered Rich Results Test pass. No claim of absent schema is based on static fetching.
- LocalBusiness uses the real locality without exposing a street address. Do not publish a private home address to satisfy rich-result validation. Check [Google’s LocalBusiness requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) separately from basic schema validity.

## On-page SEO findings

### O1. Core service snippets can be more concise and natural

**Impact:** Medium for clarity and potential click-through. **Priority:** P2 quick win.

**Evidence:** The live dog-walking and cat-sitting titles are each 72 characters after the brand template, and overnight dog sitting is 79. Their descriptions are 187, 193, and 194 characters respectively. Fox River Grove and Cary descriptions are 217 and 206 characters. Literal “near me” appears in service titles, homepage links, and service-section headings, despite the site knowing its actual location.

**Fix:** Lead with the service and real geography, retain useful price information, and move secondary qualifications into the page. Suggested rendered copy:

| Page                  | Suggested title                                         | Suggested description                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dog walking           | Dog Walking in Fox River Grove & Cary · Drew’s Pet Care | Dog walks in Fox River Grove and Cary from $22 per 30 minutes. Drew follows your dog’s pace and sends an update. Request dates and a meet & greet. |
| Cat sitting           | Cat Sitting in Fox River Grove & Cary · Drew’s Pet Care | Cat sitting at home in Fox River Grove and Cary from $22 per visit. Meals, water, litter care, and updates from Drew. Request your dates.          |
| Overnight dog sitting | Overnight Dog Sitting in Fox River Grove & Cary         | Overnight dog sitting in your Fox River Grove or Cary home from $55/night. Meals, walks, and updates from Drew. Request dates and a meet & greet.  |

These are editorial proposals, not applied changes. Account for the existing brand title template when implementing them. Replace homepage anchors “Dog walker near me” and “Cat sitter near me” with natural service labels. There is no mandatory “near me” exact-match requirement.

Character counts are a screening heuristic. Google has no fixed character limit for titles or descriptions and truncates according to display width; these observations do not prove current search-result truncation or lost CTR. See [title guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) and [snippet guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet).

### O2. Homepage H1 could identify the service and area

**Impact:** Low; the introduction and title already explain both. **Priority:** P3 editorial improvement.

**Evidence:** The live H1 is “Good care. Familiar routines. Personally, by Drew.” Service terms and the two core towns appear immediately afterward.

**Fix:** Consider “Pet sitting & dog walking in Fox River Grove & Cary” as the H1 and retain the current line nearby as supporting brand copy. Preserve readability and the current visual hierarchy. This is a clarity opportunity, not an explanation for a proven ranking loss.

## Content and local authority findings

### C1. Two overnight services have overlapping intent

**Impact:** Medium potential. **Priority:** P2. **Status:** Content overlap confirmed; ranking cannibalization unproven.

**Evidence:** `/services/house-sitting` and `/services/overnight-dog-sitting` both describe overnight stays in the customer’s home from $55, with meals, walks, updates, and an agreed daytime routine. Both are self-canonical and internally linked. House sitting’s title and H1 omit the towns while overnight dog sitting targets the local dog-sitter query.

**Fix:** Give the pages distinct jobs if both services warrant separate explanations: overnight dog sitting for dog-specific routines; house sitting for whole-household and multi-pet care using only actual offered duties. Link between them with an explicit explanation of the difference. Use Search Console query-to-page data before deciding to consolidate. If there is no meaningful difference, consider a permanent redirect to the stronger page and update links and the sitemap. Do not cross-canonical two materially different services merely to force a preferred result.

### C2. Several service pages leave practical selection questions unanswered

**Impact:** Medium. **Priority:** P2 for core services; P3 for boarding/daycare.

**Evidence:** Boarding and daycare defer the setting and most arrangements to a conversation; the page-specific information is brief relative to shared coverage and FAQ content. House sitting, drop-ins, and puppy care also largely use the shared template. This is an answer-depth assessment, not a minimum-word-count finding. The core town pages already contain distinct planning guidance and clear coverage limits.

**Fix:** First improve the services relevant to Fox River Grove and Cary: explain how owners choose a walk versus a drop-in, how overnight and daytime coverage fit together, and which routine details are agreed before care. Publish confirmed arrival-window, extra-pet, cancellation, and medication-handling policies where applicable, or link to the relevant existing terms. For boarding/daycare, explain the actual care setting, who provides care, eligibility, and practical handoff arrangements once confirmed. Do not invent facilities, credentials, capacity, or guarantees.

### C3. Independent local proof needs an account-level review

**Impact:** Potentially high for local discovery and trust. **Priority:** P1 verification, followed by ongoing work.

**Evidence:** The site identifies Drew, uses original pet-care photos, publishes contact and policy pages, and links the Google listing and Sitterfolio profile. Sitterfolio returned 200. No customer testimonials are currently published on-site. Business Profile verification, categories, reviews, duplicates, citations, and links were not independently established; the audit cannot claim they are missing or weak.

**Fix:** Review the existing profile’s verification, website and booking URLs, public phone, category, services, hours, photos, and real service areas. Check name/phone consistency on genuine existing listings before adding new ones. Ask customers neutrally for honest reviews after completed care; keep referral credits separate. Publish approved testimonials only when real feedback and permission exist. Seek useful local mentions through existing relationships with vets, groomers, and community organizations. Google describes local ranking in terms of relevance, distance, and prominence; declared service areas do not create additional physical locations. See [Google’s local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en).

### C4. Keep publishing tied to real customer questions

**Impact:** Medium over time. **Priority:** P3.

**Evidence:** One helpful holiday-preparation article is live, with an author link and links to services, rates, and booking. A daily draft workflow opens PRs for review; it does not automatically publish. The existing checks constrain invented prices, links, and raw HTML.

**Fix:** Retain human review. Prioritize questions that help owners choose and arrange actual care, with relevant links to the two core town pages where useful. Add firsthand details and approved original photos when available. A posting schedule alone is not evidence of search demand. Improve existing service and town pages before expanding into more towns or many service-by-town variants. No doorway-abuse or AI-content penalty is established here.

## Prioritized action plan

1. **Critical fixes:** None established by the live checks. Investigate any actual Search Console exclusions first.
2. **Highest-value verification:** Inspect the seven priority URLs in Search Console; review the existing Google Business Profile and analytics dashboards. Establish baseline clicks, queries, landing pages, successful inquiries, and actual clients.
3. **Quick wins:** Tighten dog-walking, cat-sitting, overnight, and core-town snippets. Use natural service anchors. Consider a descriptive homepage H1 as a lower-priority copy change.
4. **Content work:** Separate the two overnight page purposes and answer practical core-service questions. Preserve existing honest coverage and booking limitations.
5. **Ongoing:** Collect genuine reviews and approved proof, earn relevant local mentions, and publish useful firsthand guidance. Compare equivalent 28-day periods with seasonal context once data exists; do not promise a numerical uplift from this audit.
